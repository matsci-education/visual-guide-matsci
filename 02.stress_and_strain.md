## The stress tensor

Stress is not a number, and it is not a vector. Traction on a surface depends on which
way the surface faces, so the object that relates the two is a second-rank tensor. In two
dimensions:

:::{math}
:label: eq:stress-tensor
\boldsymbol{\sigma} =
\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma_{x} & \tau_{xy} \\
\tau_{xy} & \sigma_{y}
\end{pmatrix}
:::

It is symmetric — moment equilibrium on an infinitesimal element demands
$\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}$ — and that symmetry has a consequence worth dwelling on: the
eigenvalues are real, and the eigenvectors are orthogonal. There is always a rotation of
the element for which the shear stresses vanish.

:::{wolfram}
:echo: false

stressTensor[sx_, sy_, txy_] := {{sx, txy}, {txy, sy}};

principalStresses[sx_, sy_, txy_] :=
  Module[{c = (sx + sy)/2, r = Sqrt[((sx - sy)/2)^2 + txy^2]}, {c + r, c - r}];

principalAngle[sx_, sy_, txy_] := ArcTan[sx - sy, 2 txy]/2;
:::

Those eigenvalues are the *principal stresses*, and the angle that produces them is the
principal direction:

:::{wolfram}
:echo: true

Eigenvalues[stressTensor[sx, sy, txy]] // Simplify
:::

## Mohr's circle

Rewriting the rotation of a stress element in terms of the double angle $2\theta$ makes
something remarkable fall out: as the element rotates, the point
$(\sigma_{\theta}, \tau_{\theta})$ traces a circle. Its centre sits at the mean stress and
its radius is the maximum shear:

:::{math}
:label: eq:mohr
\sigma_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2},
\qquad
R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^{2} + \tau_{xy}^{2}}
:::

This is Mohr's circle, and it turns a rotation problem into a compass-and-ruler exercise.
The red diameter marks the two faces of the element as you have drawn it; the green points
where the circle crosses the $\sigma$ axis are the principal stresses.

:::{wolfram}
:echo: true
:caption: Mohr's circle. The red diameter is the current element orientation; drag θ to rotate it onto the principal axes, where the shear vanishes.

(* #| label: fig:mohr-circle *)
Manipulate[
 Module[{c, r, s1, s2, sxr, syr, txyr, m},
  c = (sx + sy)/2;
  r = Sqrt[((sx - sy)/2)^2 + txy^2];
  {s1, s2} = {c + r, c - r};
  m = RotationMatrix[-theta Degree];
  {{sxr, txyr}, {txyr, syr}} = m . stressTensor[sx, sy, txy] . Transpose[m];
  Graphics[{
    {RGBColor[0.85, 0.92, 0.98], Disk[{c, 0}, r]},
    {Gray, Dashed, Line[{{-160, 0}, {160, 0}}], Line[{{0, -110}, {0, 110}}]},
    {Thick, RGBColor[0.2, 0.4, 0.75], Circle[{c, 0}, r]},
    {Darker[Green], PointSize[0.022], Point[{{s1, 0}, {s2, 0}}]},
    {RGBColor[0.8, 0.15, 0.15], Thick,
     Line[{{sxr, txyr}, {syr, -txyr}}],
     PointSize[0.022], Point[{{sxr, txyr}, {syr, -txyr}}]},
    {Black,
     Text[Style["\[Sigma]1 = " <> ToString[NumberForm[N[s1], {4, 1}]] <>
        ", \[Sigma]2 = " <> ToString[NumberForm[N[s2], {4, 1}]], 12],
      {c, -r - 18}],
     Text[Style["R = " <> ToString[NumberForm[N[r], {4, 1}]], 12], {c, r + 14}]}
    },
   Axes -> False, PlotRange -> {{-160, 160}, {-115, 115}},
   ImageSize -> 460, AspectRatio -> Automatic]],
 {{sx, 80, "\[Sigma]x (MPa)"}, -120, 120, 1},
 {{sy, -20, "\[Sigma]y (MPa)"}, -120, 120, 1},
 {{txy, 40, "\[Tau]xy (MPa)"}, -90, 90, 1},
 {{theta, 0, "element rotation \[Theta] (\[Degree])"}, 0, 90, 1},
 SaveDefinitions -> True]
:::

Two things are worth checking by hand in @fig:mohr-circle. Set $\tau_{xy}$ to zero and the
red diameter lands on the $\sigma$ axis — the element is already principal. Then rotate
$\theta$ through 45° from that state and the red points climb to the top and bottom of the
circle: maximum shear occurs at 45° to the principal directions, which is exactly why
ductile metals in tension fail along slip planes inclined at roughly that angle.
